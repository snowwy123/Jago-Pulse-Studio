# Editing visual layers

Open **Scene & layers** above the canvas. The main Layers list now includes the scene's procedural visuals and their authored elements, alongside ordinary artwork/text layers. Masks and highlight overlays are labelled as support layers. The search field searches their names too.

Select an element such as a wordmark to reveal its direct placement controls. Drag that element on the main canvas, or enter X/Y and width/height percentages. A drag is one Undo step. The selection outline is an editing aid; exports do not include it.

**Edit scope** defaults to This scene. Choose **Matching elements across the film** to move copies with the same visual/element identity and asset; unrelated artwork is not matched by its name alone. Locked scenes are excluded. **Move its matching mask and highlight too** keeps related masks and sheen overlays aligned when repositioning. You can turn it off for independent edits. Undo restores the whole edit.

Keep when generating protects a visual from generation; direct placement in this panel remains editable. Visible in this scene toggles the selected visual. For complete visual-stack mixing, layer timing or authored shape controls, choose **Open in Visual workshop**. It opens the selected visual and element; workshop edits still use Apply/Cancel. Kept workshop layers can also be edited manually; keeping protects generation only.

The × button beside Layers closes the inspector, which is useful in a narrow window. Ordinary artwork controls and selection remain available as before.

## Lighting composition

Light architecture adds Vanishing point X/Y and Background colour wash. The Y control can lower the laser-fan origin. Tunnel defaults use a steady camera; travelling lights and musical response still work. Sideways parallax remains an explicit option for those who want it.

A broad dark colour wash can give lighting scenes a visible atmosphere without a large flat black plate. Scanlines are a separate artistic finish; they are not a substitute for checking levels and gradients on the destination display. This tool does not guarantee calibration for an LED wall.
